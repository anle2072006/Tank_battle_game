\section{Task 1: N-Queens -- Propositional Logic and Local Search}

\subsection{Propositional formulation}
Let $x_{r,c}$ be true iff a queen is on row $r$, column $c$ ($N^2$ variables).
The constraints, all in CNF, are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item every row and every column has at least one queen: $\bigvee_{c} x_{r,c}$ and $\bigvee_{r} x_{r,c}$;
  \item no two queens share a line: for any two distinct squares $a,b$ on the same row,
        column or diagonal, the clause $(\neg a \lor \neg b)$ (i.e.\ $a \rightarrow \neg b$).
\end{itemize}
The formula has $2N + 2N\binom{N}{2} + 2\bigl(2\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\binom{k}{2}+\binom{N}{2}\bigr)$
clauses, i.e.\ $\Theta(N^3)$. The satisfying assignment is decoded from the model returned by
Glucose3 (PySAT) and checked independently.

\subsection{Min-Conflicts with a variable-selection heuristic}
The state is $\text{queens}[c]=r$ (one queen per column). Row and diagonal counters give the
conflicts of any square in $O(1)$. At each step a conflicted column is chosen and moved to the row
with the fewest conflicts (random tie-breaking); restarts are used after a step limit.
We compare three variable-selection rules: \emph{random} (standard), \emph{max-conflict}, and the
proposed \emph{oldest} rule that scores a conflicted column by
$\text{conflicts}\times(1+\text{age}/N)$, where age is the number of steps since the column was last moved.
We also compare random initialisation with a greedy (min-conflict) initialisation.

\subsection{Discussion}
Clauses grow cubically but Glucose3 solves all tested instances ($N\le 50$) in well under a
second. Min-Conflicts scales to $N=1000$ in $\approx0.2$\,s. Greedy initialisation cuts the mean steps
at $N=1000$ from $\approx 700$ to $\approx 80$, and the \emph{oldest} rule further reduces steps
(about $49$ vs.\ $82$ for random selection with greedy init). All runs succeeded.
